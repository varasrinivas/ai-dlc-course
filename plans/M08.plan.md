## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Run a Mob Construction review: AI presents the increment against its plan, deviations
   first; the named decider disposes — sign-off or send-back with a decision.
2. Apply the understand-every-line discipline with concrete techniques: the AI
   walk-through, ask-me-anything sampling, and spot-explain challenges.
3. Order the gate stack correctly: mechanical gates (tests, lint, build) run before
   humans look — human attention is spent only on what machines can't check.
4. Write a send-back that converges: a decision attached, not a vibe ("don't like it").

## Hook

Friday, 13:00. UW-1a's increment is up on the screen: 14 files, 312 lines, tests green.
The AI opens the way it always must — deviations first: "Two departures from the approved
plan: I added a serviceCode format check that wasn't in step 1, and the outreach log
writes through a buffer, not directly." The architect points at a line: "Explain the
buffer flush condition." Thirty seconds later the room knows something subtle about
failure ordering that no one would have caught scrolling a GitHub diff at 4 pm. That's
Mob Construction: not a meeting about code, but the moment the team either understands
its system or merges a stranger into it.

## Concept outline

1. **The increment review, as a ritual** (`<h2>`)
   - Structure mirrors M06: one increment at a time, AI presents its own work against
     the approved plan — deviations first, then the criteria walk (fig 7.2 arrows),
     then open floor.
   - The room: the named decider must attend (M03's table); engineers who'll build
     adjacent UWs attend because this diff is their tomorrow's context.
   - Deviations aren't sins — unexplained deviations are; a deviation with a reason is
     the AI exercising judgment transparently.
2. **Understand every line** (`<h2>`)
   - The rule: nothing merges that no human present can explain — in regulated code,
     "the AI wrote it" is not an answer an auditor accepts.
   - Techniques that scale: the AI walk-through (author explains, humans interrupt);
     spot-explain sampling (pick 3 random hunks, a human explains them back);
     ask-me-anything (any line, 30-second answer required).
   - The M03 sizing rule is the enabler: 312 lines is understandable; 4,200 is theater.
3. **Gates and send-backs** (`<h2>`)
   - The gate stack: mechanical first (tests green, lint clean, build passes — machines
     gate before humans spend attention); human gates verify what machines can't:
     criteria coverage, constraint compliance, explanation quality. Hook/automation
     mechanics in M17.
   - Two exits only: sign-off, or send-back *with a decision* — "extract the flush
     threshold to config and name it" converges; "clean this up" loops forever.
   - Every disposition lands in the increment summary → context memory; a send-back
     re-enters the construction loop as a narrow re-plan, not a restart.

## Recap bullets

1. Mob Construction mirrors Mob Elaboration: one increment at a time, the AI presents
   its own work — deviations first — and the named decider disposes.
2. Understand every line is enforceable: walk-throughs, spot-explain sampling,
   ask-me-anything — sized by M03's rule so it stays honest.
3. Machines gate first (tests, lint, build); humans gate what machines can't — criteria,
   constraints, explanations.
4. Two exits only: sign-off, or send-back with a decision attached; both land in context
   memory.
