## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Build the three-tier scorecard: promise metrics (the intent's clauses), flow
   metrics (bolt lead time, decision latency), health metrics (gate catch rate,
   escaped defects, test fidelity).
2. Spot vanity metrics by their tell — a numerator with no member or business
   outcome — and name what to measure instead.
3. Apply Goodhart discipline: every target gets a guard metric that detects gaming.
4. Treat infrastructure as a precondition, not a nice-to-have: CI/CD and test
   fidelity are what make bolt-speed real rather than theatrical (M04's conditions,
   operationalized).

## Hook

Two dashboards land on the CTO's desk in the same week. Vendor A's: "34,000 AI-
generated lines merged, 87% suggestion acceptance, 41 hours of AI usage per
developer." The portal team's: "median auto-approvable turnaround 3.1 hours against
a 4.2-day baseline and a 6% counterfactual; bolt lead time 2.3 days; skip-alarm
zero fires; gates caught 4, rubber-stamped 2 (fixed)." The first dashboard measures
motion and can be inflated by writing worse code faster. The second measures a
promise kept, the speed of keeping it, and whether the system that keeps it is
healthy — and every number on it can be walked back to artifacts. Measurement is a
leadership artifact like any other: it either traces, or it decorates.

## Concept outline

1. **Measure the promise, not the motion** (`<h2>`)
   - Tier 1 — promise: the intent's own clauses as standing metrics (turnaround vs
     24h; zero below-threshold skips; urgent SLA) — with baseline frozen before
     bolt 1 and the counterfactual guard (M04's bar, now program policy).
   - Tier 2 — flow: bolt lead time (intent-to-shipped per bolt), decision latency
     (M29's number — the tempo-setter measured directly), close-out completeness.
   - Adoption measured as behavior, not sentiment: % of changes flowing through the
     full artifact chain (Blue Origin's 95% was this shape — as reported).
2. **Vanity metrics and their tells** (`<h2>`)
   - The rogues' gallery: LOC generated, suggestion-acceptance rate, AI usage
     hours, velocity-point inflation — each pumpable while the member waits just
     as long.
   - The tell: a numerator with no member or business outcome attached; the test:
     "could this number improve while the portal gets worse?" If yes, it's decor.
   - Goodhart discipline: any metric made a target gets a guard — gate pass rate
     targeted alone breeds rubber stamps (M17), so it pairs with catch rate;
     turnaround pairs with the skip-alarm (speed never buys past the guardrail).
3. **Quality signals and the infrastructure floor** (`<h2>`)
   - Tier 3 — health: gate catch rate (fired/rubber-stamped/caught per close),
     escaped defects per bolt, audit-walk completeness sampling, test fidelity —
     AC-named coverage, not percentage theater (fig 7.2's arrows as a metric).
   - The infrastructure floor is M04's conditions operationalized: without CI/CD
     that deploys per bolt and tests that actually trace, bolt speed is theater —
     measure the floor before bragging about the ceiling.
   - Reporting cadence: the numbers fall out of close-outs — the scorecard is
     curation, not a new bureaucracy; if a metric needs manual collection, suspect
     it.

desk, the inspector (cameo), a rival developer. Draft (≈105 words):

> The developer two parcels over measures his sites by truckloads of lumber
> delivered per week, and his foremen have learned to order early and stack high.
> Meera's dashboard for the street has three lines per lot: days from signed

> catch and escaped-defect signals · **"count what the family moves into"** =
> promise metrics traced to the intent

## Recap bullets

1. Three tiers that trace: promise (the intent's clauses), flow (bolt lead time,
   decision latency), health (gate catches, escaped defects, test fidelity).
2. The vanity tell: a numerator with no member outcome — if it can improve while
   the portal gets worse, it's decoration.
3. Goodhart discipline: every target carries a guard; speed never buys past the
   skip-alarm, and gate pass rate never travels without catch rate.
4. Infrastructure is the floor: per-bolt CI/CD and AC-named test fidelity are what
   make the speed real — measure the floor first.
5. Next → M32 · The Rollout Playbook — the last module.
