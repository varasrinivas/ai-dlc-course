## Hook

The South team takes UW-R1 — the outreach-event query from the reporting epic — and
the session looks familiar by design: plan, approval, build in steps, gates green,
increment summary. Two things are new. The mechanical sweep mid-loop (renaming a
DTO across 14 files) runs in full-auto inside the sandbox: no network, nothing outside
the repo, blast radius bounded by construction. And at review, the南 reviewer ignores
the engine's tidy summary and reads the raw diff — where she finds a debug log line
printing `memberId`. The summary didn't mention it. Summaries never do. The diff is
where the truth lives, on every engine, and the discipline of reading it is what
Codex's contractual gates are made of.

## Concept outline

1. **The build loop in Codex** (`<h2>`)
   - M16 verbatim: one UW per session, enter reading the chain, plan-first contract,
     build stepwise, exit writing artifacts.
   - Posture per phase: suggest for consequential slices (every change warm-approved),
     auto-edit for tight trusted loops — with the M17 gate scripts as the standing
     backstop either way.
   - The increment contract is engine-agnostic: AC-named tests, deviations flagged as
     made, summary at the end. Reviewers shouldn't be able to tell which engine built
     it from the artifacts alone.
2. **The sandbox** (`<h2>`)
   - What full-auto's sandbox is: execution with network disabled and writes scoped
     to the working directory — containment as the counterweight to autonomy (M19).
   - When it earns its keep: rigor-policy low-risk classes and big mechanical sweeps
     (the 14-file rename) where prompting per step is fatigue without safety.
   - What it does NOT replace: review. A sandboxed diff is a contained diff, not a
     correct one — the guardrail classes still run suggest + human gates.
3. **Diff review discipline** (`<h2>`)
   - The rule: read the diff, not the summary — summaries are the author's theory of
     the diff; the log line printing memberId lives in the hunks.
   - Structure the read like M08: deviations first (ask for them), then criteria walk,
     then the cold read of anything unexplained; git is the checkpoint ledger — one
     commit per approved increment, revert as the cheap undo.
   - The Codex-specific edge: with contractual enforcement, the reviewer IS the hook —
     what PreToolUse blocks mechanically on Claude Code, the suggest-posture reviewer
     blocks by reading. Budget attention accordingly (M17's fatigue economics).

## Analogy (mandatory) — the dust wall

Placement: after concept section 2. Cast: South crew (their foreman), Meera & Ravi.
Advances T5: the South crew's build style. Draft (≈100 words):

> The South crew hung plastic sheeting before demolition week — a dust wall sealing
> the work zone from the finished wing. Inside it they moved fast: saws, dust,
> mistakes, all contained; nothing they did in there could stain the oiled floors
> beyond the plastic. But the wall had one zippered door, and the rule was absolute:
> every board, every fixture passed through it got looked at — in hand, in the light,
> not from the invoice. Ravi once tried to wave a trim bundle through on the
> paperwork. The foreman unzipped it anyway: two warped lengths, top of the stack.
> "Invoices lie politely," she said. "Boards don't."
>
> `maps to →` **the dust wall** = the sandbox: fast and messy inside, nothing leaks
> out · **the zippered door** = diffs applied only through review · **in hand, not
> from the invoice** = read the diff, not the summary · **the warped lengths** = what
> summaries politely omit
