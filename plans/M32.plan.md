## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Sequence a rollout: pilot with real stakes and an evidence bar → scale by seeding
   pilot alumni into new pods → sustain via charter, scorecard, and the chain — with
   explicit gate criteria between phases.
2. Design enablement that respects engineers: hands-on bolts over slide decks,
   objections answered with mechanisms, opt-in early and standard later.
3. Name the four rollout killers — mandates before evidence, tool-first thinking,
   vanity dashboards, skipping the infrastructure floor — and their antidotes.
4. State the closing strategic frame: the methodology is the durable asset; engines
   are replaceable; people carry the practice.

## Hook

Eighteen months from the funding email, the CTO asks for the thing every successful
pilot eventually owes the org: "Make it everyone's." And the temptation, with the
portal's numbers on the wall, is the memo — *effective next quarter, all teams
will...* The team that built the portal knows better, because they remember what
actually convinced them: not a mandate, not a demo, but the Tuesday they watched a
question get answered in four minutes that would have been a two-week ticket. You
can't memo that. You can only let people build one bolt inside a system that works,
then hand them the book and let them seed the next site. That's the playbook, and
it's the last thing this course teaches: transformations don't roll out. They
propagate.

## Concept outline

1. **Pilot → scale → sustain** (`<h2>`)
   - Pilot: one pod, real stakes (never a toy project — the portal worked because a
     member's wait was real), the M04 evidence bar set before bolt 1; duration in
     bolts, not quarters.
   - Scale by seeding: pilot alumni split to anchor new pods (the practice travels
     in people + the chain, not in decks); each new pod inherits the charter, the
     engine policy, the stage library — and writes its own decisions.
   - Sustain: the operating system is already built — charter (M30), scorecard
     (M31), the chain; gate criteria between phases: don't seed until the pilot's
     floor metrics hold; don't standardize until two seeded pods hit the bar
     unassisted.
2. **Enablement that respects engineers** (`<h2>`)
   - Engineers change practice through practice: enablement is a facilitated first
     bolt on the team's own backlog — not a slide deck, not a certification quiz;
     the dual-path design (leaders trace artifacts, builders run engines) is this
     course's own architecture, reusable.
   - Objections are signal, met with mechanism: "AI will write garbage" → the gate
     stack and understand-every-line; "I'll become a prompt babysitter" → the role
     shifts toward judgment (M29); "my expertise is obsolete" → the SME's
     jurisdiction got stronger, not weaker (M26).
   - Opt-in early, standard later: volunteers make the pilot honest; the standard
     arrives only after the evidence, with an exception path that costs a
     conversation, not a career.
3. **The leader's long game** (`<h2>`)
   - A quarter map: Q1 pilot pod + evidence bar; Q2 seed two pods + charter +
     engine policy org-wide; Q3 program scorecard + master-permit negotiation with
     compliance; Q4 standard-with-exceptions + enablement pipeline running itself.
   - The four killers: mandates before evidence (adoption theater), tool-first
     thinking (buying engines without the methodology — M22 inverted), vanity
     dashboards (M31's rogues), skipping the floor (bolt speed on a quarterly
     release train).
   - The closing frame: engines will be replaced — twice, probably, before the
     methodology needs revisiting; what compounds is the chain, the rituals, and
     the people who've learned to decide fast with recorded rationale. Build those.

> The street finished fourteen months after the first survey stake — twelve houses,
> each with its own build book, each book opening with the same front section. The
> families who moved in never met the North crew's foreman, but they live inside

> desk. The desk's reply went in every build book on the street: "You didn't buy

## Lab outline

  all teams adopt AI development, 50% of code AI-generated by Q3, weekly AI-usage
  reports, mandatory 2-hour training"). Trace: identify all four killers in one
  memo; rewrite its first paragraph as propagation. 3 check questions + answers.
- **Path B — Lead It (decision exercise):** write your org's four-quarter rollout

  (2) the two phase gates with their criteria; (3) enablement design (the
  facilitated-first-bolt program + the three objections you expect and the
  mechanism answering each); (4) sustain mechanisms (who owns charter, scorecard,
  and the exception path). Compare against a worked answer.
