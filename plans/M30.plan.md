## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Make the central reframe: AI-DLC's oversight *is* the governance — named deciders,
   recorded rationale, mechanical gates — governance by construction, not
   documentation after the fact.
2. Stand up the regulated stack: PHI-review stage, guardrail gates that can't be
   dialed down, immutable audit surfaces, evidence packs per change class.
3. Answer the auditor's three questions (who decided, what changed, who verified)
   from artifacts alone — and the regulator's AI question ("who is accountable?")
   with a register that never says "the AI decided."
4. Scale governance without strangling: rigor classes as the governance contract,
   pod self-governance within classes, leadership audit at bolt closes.

## Hook

The external auditors arrive in March expecting the usual two weeks of archaeology —
screenshots, email trails, engineers reconstructing decisions from memory. They get
something else: a stage library that says what the process is, per-bolt logs that
show it ran, a decision register where every consequential call has a name and a
rationale, and an audit walk they can run themselves on any determination. The lead
auditor's summary sentence is the one worth building for: "Usually we audit what
teams say they do. Here we audited what the system can't help but record." The
governance didn't slow the portal down. It's made of the same parts that made the
portal fast.

## Concept outline

1. **Oversight is the governance** (`<h2>`)
   - The reframe: checkpoints, named deciders, recorded rationale, and gates weren't
     compliance overhead added to AI-DLC — they ARE the compliance system;
     governance by construction beats governance by documentation-after.
   - Every mechanism does double duty: M02's decision table = delegation of
     authority matrix; decisions.md = the decision register; M17 hooks = preventive
     controls; the close-out = the control-effectiveness report.
   - The cultural consequence: teams stop experiencing governance as a foreign tax —
     the fastest path and the compliant path are the same path.
2. **The regulated stack (healthcare/PHI)** (`<h2>`)
   - The non-negotiables, named: the PHI-review stage on guardrail-class changes
     (M11), the threshold lock (M17), immutable scores and determinations (B2/B4),
     the audit walk (M27), synthetic-only fixtures (M14).
   - Accountability structure: the decision register names humans — "the AI decided"
     appears nowhere, because plan→ask→defer makes it structurally impossible for a
     consequential decision to lack an owner.
   - What auditors ask → what artifacts answer: who decided (register), what changed
     (increments + summaries), who verified (sign-offs + gate logs), how do you know
     it still holds (alarms + reconciliation).
3. **Scaling governance without strangling** (`<h2>`)
   - The failure mode to fear now: AI anxiety re-imports governance theater — a
     committee for every prompt. The antidote is the rigor policy AS the governance
     contract: classes and their gates negotiated once with compliance, then pods
     self-govern within them.
   - Delegation with audit: pods run their own checkpoints; leadership audits
     close-outs (M17/M29's gate audit) — trust the mechanism, verify the audit
     trail.
   - The regulator's AI question answered in advance: models don't hold authority;
     humans at named checkpoints do; the chain proves which human, when, and why.

## Analogy (mandatory) — the master permit

Placement: after concept section 2. Cast: Meera & Ravi (developers), the inspector,
the design desk, the build book. Draft (≈105 words):

> Twelve houses meant the county, and Meera expected war. Instead the inspector
> proposed a master permit: standard inspection points across every lot, agreed
> once — because he'd worked the lake house and knew the firm's books. "I don't
> inspect harder where the records are this good," he said. "I inspect smarter."
> Site teams passed most checks with the book open on the tailgate; the inspector
> spot-audited two lots a month, deeply. But one item survived every negotiation,
> written in the permit's first line: anything touching the waterline gets the
> wet-stamp, every time, no volume discounts. Ravi signed it without arguing.
> Some gates are the reason the rest can be light.
>
> `maps to →` **the master permit** = rigor classes negotiated once as the
> governance contract · **inspect smarter, not harder** = spot-audit close-outs,
> trust the mechanism · **book on the tailgate** = artifacts as standing evidence ·
> **the wet-stamp, every time** = PHI/guardrail gates that never dial down
