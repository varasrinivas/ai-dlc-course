## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Define a bolt precisely — one Inception → Construction → Operations pass in hours-to-days
   — and explain why it is not just a shorter sprint.
2. State the sizing rule for a unit of work: one AI construction loop, one human checkpoint,
   independently reviewable.
3. Describe the anatomy of Mob Elaboration and Mob Construction: when each runs, who is in
   the room, what the AI brings, and each ritual's exit criteria.
4. Trace context memory across a bolt: which artifacts get written (decisions, plans,
   steering updates) and how the next bolt starts smarter because of them.

## Hook

Tuesday, 8:55 am. The team's old calendar has collided with its new tempo: today holds a
"sprint planning" block that was sized for two weeks of intake work — and the intake plan
was validated, built, and reviewed by 4 pm Monday. The Scrum master flips through the
ceremony list asking what survives: standup? retro? refinement? The honest answer is that
the ceremonies were load-bearing for a world where construction was the slow part, and that
world just ended. AI-DLC doesn't tweak the old rituals; it replaces them — and it renames
them on purpose, so nobody imports two-week habits into a cycle measured in hours.

## Concept outline

1. **Why the words change** (`<h2>`)
   - Renaming is a commitment device: "bolt" blocks the muscle memory of sprint ceremonies;
     new tempo needs new defaults (M00's bridge table said what maps to what; this module
     says how each new thing actually runs).
   - A bolt is one full Inception → Construction → Operations pass, in hours to days —
     not a compressed sprint: the phases recur per bolt, not per quarter.
   - Tempo consequence (from M02): coordination is the tempo-setter, so rituals exist to
     make decisions arrive fast, not to report status.
2. **Units of work: the sizing rule** (`<h2>`)
   - Definition: the smallest slice sized for one AI construction loop ending in one human
     checkpoint; independently reviewable; small enough to be understood line by line.
   - Prior Auth worked example: "intake" is an epic; UW-1 schema + eligibility validation,
     UW-2 criteria scoring, UW-3 review-queue routing, UW-4 determinations (echoes M00's
     Path A transcript — deliberate continuity).
   - Too big / too small tells: needs two checkpoints → split; can't be reviewed alone →
     merge or re-slice.
3. **The two mob rituals** (`<h2>`)
   - Mob Elaboration (Inception): AI presents drafted requirements/stories + its clarifying
     questions; whole team — PO, architect, SMEs — validates and answers live; exit when
     units of work + acceptance criteria are approved. (Practice run in M06.)
   - Mob Construction (Construction): AI presents a built increment + its plan deviations;
     team reviews at the checkpoint, understand-every-line discipline; exit when a human
     signs off or sends it back with a decision. (Practice run in M08.)
   - "Mob" is the point: the decision-owners are already in the room, so deferring to a
     human costs minutes, not a ticket queue.
4. **Context memory: the artifact chain** (`<h2>`)
   - What gets written per bolt: the plan, the decision log, steering-file updates —
     durable, readable by both engines and by humans.
   - Why it compounds: bolt N+1's Mob Elaboration starts from bolt N's decisions instead
     of re-litigating them; the M02 lab's `docs/decisions.md` line was this in miniature.
   - One sentence of restraint: how context is engineered (semantic maps, steering
     patterns) is Track 3's whole business — here, just the chain.

> By the second week, Meera and Ravi stopped asking "when will the house be done?" The
> site had a rhythm instead of a schedule: each morning the design desk opened with
> questions, each crew took exactly one work package — never two — and each evening ended
> with a walkthrough where the owners signed off or sent something back. Anything decided
> on foot went into the build book before the crews left. The firm never said "phase."
> It said "today's package." Progress stopped being a date on a chart and became a pile
> of signed-off days.
>
> `maps to →` **one work package per day** = a unit of work per bolt · **the evening
> walkthrough** = Mob Construction's checkpoint · **the morning questions** = Mob
> Elaboration · **decided-on-foot → build book** = context memory

## Bridge table

Ritual-anatomy table (`table.bridge`) — deliberately deeper than M00's mapping table.
Columns: **Ritual / When it runs / Who's in the room / Exit criteria**:
| Mob Elaboration | Inception, opening the bolt | Whole team + AI (PO, architect, SMEs) | Units of work + acceptance criteria approved by humans |
| Mob Construction | Construction, at each checkpoint | Team + AI; the named decider must attend | Increment understood line-by-line; sign-off or a decision sent back |
| Bolt close | Operations, end of bolt | Whoever shipped it | Deployed/observable; decisions and plans written to context memory |

## Recap bullets

1. A bolt is one full Inception → Construction → Operations pass in hours to days — the
   phases recur per bolt, and the renaming is a deliberate break from sprint muscle memory.
2. A unit of work is sized by the checkpoint: one AI construction loop, one human decision,
   independently reviewable.
3. Mob Elaboration opens the bolt (team validates AI-drafted requirements); Mob
   Construction guards it (understand every line before sign-off).
4. Context memory is the artifact chain — plans, decisions, steering updates — that makes
   bolt N+1 start smarter than bolt N.
