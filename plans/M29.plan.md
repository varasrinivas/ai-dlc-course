## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Design a pod: small, cross-functional, sized so every decision-owner fits in one
   room — the mob rituals' feasibility condition turned into an org rule.
2. Replace the sprint ceremony calendar with the bolt ritual calendar, and name what
   dies (status standups, sprint planning, batch retros).
3. Coach the role shifts: PO as decision-owner on call, architect as contract pinner
   and seam judge, QA as gate designer and spot-explain auditor.
4. Protect the two things leaders must protect: decision latency and checkpoint
   integrity.

## Hook

The portal pod's numbers reach the VP of engineering, and her first question is
organizational: "So do I reorg everyone into pods now?" The honest answer from the
pilot: the pod wasn't a boxes-and-lines exercise — it was derived from one
constraint. Every consequential decision needed its owner in the room within minutes,
not tickets. Work backwards from that and the topology designs itself: five people,
three jurisdictions, one SME on a hotline, two engines. The ceremonies didn't need
abolishing; they starved — a status standup can't survive contact with a decision
log everyone already read. What leaders own in this model isn't the process. It's
the two numbers the process lives or dies on: how fast decisions arrive, and whether
checkpoints stay real.

## Concept outline

1. **Pods over squads** (`<h2>`)
   - The sizing rule is M06 inverted: a pod is as large as the room in which every
     decision-owner can convene in minutes — decider (PO-shaped), 2–4 engineers,
     SME access with jurisdiction, engines per the routing policy.
   - Contrast the squad/chapter matrix: optimized for skill utilization and
     coordination-by-ticket; the pod optimizes for decision latency — because M02
     made coordination the tempo-setter.
   - Pods scale by splitting on decision-domain seams (the street: one pod per
     house), never by adding people until the room stops working.
2. **The ritual calendar** (`<h2>`)
   - Per bolt: Mob Elaboration opens, Mob Construction guards, bolt close writes —
     the whole calendar; cadence follows bolts, not weeks.
   - What dies and why: status standups (the chain already told everyone), sprint
     planning (Inception does it per bolt, better), batch retros (learnings land at
     every bolt close, not quarterly archaeology).
   - The leader's calendar job: protect the timeboxes (M06's decision rule needs
     enforcement air cover) and keep deciders unblocked — a decider in back-to-back
     meetings is a stalled bolt.
3. **Role shifts** (`<h2>`)
   - PO: backlog groomer → decision-owner on call — jurisdiction explicit, latency
     measured, rationale recorded.
   - Architect: document writer → contract pinner and seam judge (M18's chalk);
     QA: test author → gate designer and spot-explain auditor (owns the gate stack's
     health, samples understanding at reviews).
   - The EM/leader: velocity tracker → checkpoint-topology owner — audits which
     gates fire, which rubber-stamp, which catch (M17's bolt-close audit, now a
     leadership artifact). The scarce skill everywhere: deciding fast with recorded
     rationale.

> The lake house turned out so well that Meera and Ravi bought the parcel next door —

> **the owner's proxy on site** = the decision-owner on call · **the weekly meeting
> that died** = status ceremonies starved by the chain · **Meera's two numbers** =
> decision latency and checkpoint integrity
