## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. State AI-DLC's two dimensions precisely: AI-powered execution with human oversight, and
   dynamic team collaboration.
2. Describe the plan → ask → defer protocol and identify each move in a working transcript.
3. Classify a given decision as AI-proposable vs human-critical (blast radius, reversibility,
   regulatory exposure) using Prior Auth Portal examples.
4. Explain why oversight in AI-DLC is checkpoint-shaped, not micromanagement-shaped — and
   what the team does differently between checkpoints (collaboration reorganizes around
   decision moments, not handoffs).

## Hook

Monday, 9:04 am, the portal rebuild's first working session. The AI has already read the
domain brief and posted a proposed plan for the intake slice — and three questions: does an
`AuthRequest` for an ineligible `Member` get rejected at intake or logged for outreach?
Are urgent requests a separate intake path or a priority flag? And who may change
`AUTO_APPROVE_THRESHOLD`? The nurse-review SME laughs: "It's asking better questions than
our last vendor." The architect is warier: "If it drafts the plan, who's actually deciding?"
That question — who decides, and exactly when — is the whole of AI-DLC's two principles,
and this module answers it.

## Concept outline

1. **Dimension one: AI-powered execution with human oversight** (`<h2>`)
   - The inversion: AI drives the work end to end — drafts requirements, plans,
     architecture, code; humans steer at defined moments. Not AI helping humans type;
     humans supervising AI that executes.
   - The protocol: the AI **plans** (complete, reviewable proposals), **asks** (clarifying
     questions where intent is ambiguous), **defers** (critical decisions are queued for
     humans, never assumed — the exact failure M01's triage bot committed).
   - Oversight is checkpoint-shaped: consequential decisions get a named human decider;
     between checkpoints the AI moves at full speed.
2. **Which decisions are "critical"?** (`<h2>`)
   - Classification heuristics: blast radius, reversibility, regulatory exposure.
   - Prior Auth examples across the spectrum: `AUTO_APPROVE_THRESHOLD` semantics,
     rejected-vs-outreach intake policy = human-critical; DTO field naming, test
     scaffolding layout = AI-proposable.
   - Foreshadow (one sentence): tuning how much rigor a change deserves is adaptive
     rigor, Track 3.
3. **Dimension two: dynamic team collaboration** (`<h2>`)
   - Teams reorganize around decision moments instead of handoffs: whoever the decision
     needs — PO, architect, nurse SME — convenes when the AI surfaces it, then disperses.
   - Contrast with handoff choreography (ticket → analyst → dev → QA); AI compresses
     execution so coordination, not construction, becomes the tempo-setter.
   - Names the rituals (Mob Elaboration, Mob Construction) as the structured form of
     this — defined fully in M03. Keep to a pointer, don't teach the rituals here.

## Recap bullets

1. Dimension one — AI-powered execution with human oversight: the AI drives the whole
   lifecycle; humans steer at checkpoints.
2. The protocol is plan → ask → defer: complete proposals, clarifying questions, and
   critical decisions queued for a named human — never assumed.
3. Critical = high blast radius, hard to reverse, or regulated; everything else the AI
   proposes and moves on.
4. Dimension two — dynamic team collaboration: the team convenes around decision moments,
   not handoffs; Mob Elaboration and Mob Construction (M03) are its structured form.
5. Next → M03 · New Rituals & Vocabulary.
