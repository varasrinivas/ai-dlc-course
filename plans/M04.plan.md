## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Cite the three AWS-reported results accurately (Bedrock 18-month/30-dev estimate
   delivered by 6 engineers in 76 days; Amazon Stores 4.5x developer velocity; Blue Origin
   95% adoption) — with the "as reported" qualifier attached.
2. Name the enabling conditions AWS credits — small modules, monorepo context, explicit
   errors — and map each onto the AI-DLC concept it validates.
3. Distinguish outcome claims from enabling conditions when reading a vendor or internal
   pitch deck.
4. Define what evidence their own Prior Auth pilot must produce before scaling (baseline,
   metric, counterfactual guard) — the skill M31 deepens.

## Hook

The portal team's first bolts are working, and now the program needs funding to scale —
which means Thursday's meeting with the CFO and the chief medical officer. The CFO has seen
a decade of "10x" slideware and has one question ready: "Show me this working somewhere
real, at scale, with numbers someone signed their name to." What exists is AWS's reported
record for AI-DLC: one re-architecture, one velocity figure, one adoption rate. Your job on
Thursday is to present those numbers honestly — including what made them possible and what
they don't prove about your org — because the fastest way to lose a CFO is to get caught
inflating someone else's results.

## Concept outline

1. **Three results, as reported** (`<h2>`)
   - Bedrock re-architecture: estimated at 18 months for 30 developers; delivered by 6
     engineers in 76 days — the flagship compression story.
   - Amazon Stores: 4.5x developer velocity; Blue Origin: 95% adoption — one throughput
     claim, one behavior-change claim; note they measure different things.
   - Epistemics without cynicism: self-reported by the methodology's author; "as reported
     by AWS" is how a credible leader cites it.
2. **What made those work** (`<h2>`)
   - Small modules → the unit-of-work sizing rule (M03) at codebase scale: slices an AI
     can hold and a human can review.
   - Monorepo context → context memory (M03): the AI could see the whole system;
     foreshadow semantic context building for messier estates (Track 3).
   - Explicit errors → loud, checkable failure over silent wrongness: the validation-gate
     habit (inspector cameo; gates deepen in M17 and Track 7).
   - The point: these are *conditions*, not luck — and they are buildable.
3. **Reading evidence like a leader** (`<h2>`)
   - Outcome claims vs enabling conditions: the pitch-deck failure mode is quoting 76 days
     while skipping the conditions that produced it.
   - What your pilot must produce: a baseline, one honest metric, and a counterfactual
     guard ("would this team have sped up anyway?") — vanity-metric warning, deepened
     in M31.
   - Calibrated pitch: promise the conditions, report the outcomes you measure.
