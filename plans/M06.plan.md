## Objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Set up a Mob Elaboration session: who must attend, what the AI brings, what the
   timebox protects.
2. Run the clarifying-question loop: ask → bounded discussion → decide → restate → record,
   and keep it from collapsing into an open-ended meeting.
3. Handle the two failure moments: live disagreement between decision-owners, and
   questions nobody in the room can answer (parking with an owner and a date).
4. Write acceptance criteria that hold: testable, traceable to a requirement, edge-case
   bearing — for the intake epic specifically.

## Hook

Wednesday, 10:00. One conference room, six people, and the AI's Inception artifacts on the
big screen: nine requirements, three stories, six open questions on the intake epic. The
meeting invite says 50 minutes, and the facilitator opens with the only rule that matters:
"We are here to decide, not to discuss. Anything we can't decide in three minutes gets an
owner and a date." By 10:50 the intake epic will either have validated units of work with
acceptance criteria — or the team will have discovered that Mob Elaboration is just
backlog grooming with better slides. The difference is technique, and technique is
learnable.

## Concept outline

1. **The room and the roles** (`<h2>`)
   - Required: every decision-owner the artifacts touch — PO (policy), architect
     (integration), nurse SME (clinical semantics); engineers who'll take the bolts.
     The AI attends as presenter, not stenographer: it drafted everything on screen.
   - The facilitator protects two things: the timebox and the decision rule (three
     minutes → decide or park with owner + date).
   - What "validation" means: humans approve, amend, or reject each artifact — silence
     is not approval; each artifact gets an explicit disposition.
2. **The clarifying-question loop** (`<h2>`)
   - Five stages per question: AI asks (with its best guess and why it can't just pick) →
     bounded discussion → a named human decides → AI restates the decision as a
     constraint → recorded in decisions.md before the next question.
   - Disagreement is signal: nurse SME vs PO on urgent-request handling is the room
     working correctly — the facilitator narrows to the deciding authority, not consensus.
   - The parking lot is a promise, not a graveyard: parked = owner + date + which UW it
     blocks; a parked question that blocks bolt 1 reorders the backlog.
3. **Acceptance criteria that hold** (`<h2>`)
   - Three tests: testable (a machine or a reviewer can verify), traceable (names its
     requirement), edge-bearing (encodes at least the known edges — below-threshold,
     ineligible Member, out-of-network Provider).
   - Worked example on the intake story; before/after: "intake validates requests
     properly" → three verifiable clauses.
   - Criteria are the Construction contract: M07's plans and tests trace to them.

## Recap bullets

1. Mob Elaboration is a decision meeting: every decision-owner present, AI presenting its
   own drafts, facilitator protecting the timebox and the decision rule.
2. The question loop has five stages — ask, bounded discussion, named decision, restate,
   record — and runs one question at a time.
3. Disagreement is the room working; the facilitator finds the deciding authority, not
   consensus. Unanswerable questions get parked with an owner, a date, and the UW they block.
4. Acceptance criteria must be testable, traceable, and edge-bearing — they are the
   contract Construction builds against (M07).
5. Next → M07 · Construction: Plans, Code, Tests.
